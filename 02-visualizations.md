# Power BI Reporting & Visualization – Day 2

## 📌 Overview

Today I started learning how to build **Power BI reports after cleaning and preparing data**.

The main focus of today's learning was understanding different Power BI visualizations, when to use them, how to apply filters, and how to analyze data using univariate, bivariate, and multivariate analysis.

---

## 📚 Topics Learned Today

### 1. Power BI Report, Visual & Dashboard

* **Visual:** A single chart or visualization in Power BI.
* **Report:** A collection of visuals organized into one or more pages.
* **Dashboard:** A single-page view containing important visuals/tiles used to monitor business performance.
* **Tile:** A visual pinned to a Power BI dashboard.

---

## 📊 2. Bar / Column Chart

Bar and column charts are mainly used to **compare categorical data with numerical data**.

### Example

**Sales by Country**

* Category → Country
* Numerical value → Sales

They can be used for:

* Sales by country
* Profit by product
* Orders by customer
* Revenue by department

---

## 🔎 3. Filters

Filters help us focus on specific data without changing the underlying dataset.

### Examples

* Top 5 customers by sales
* Bottom 5 customers by orders
* Sales for a particular country
* Revenue for a specific year

### Types of filters

* Visual-level filter
* Page-level filter
* Report-level filter

---

## 📈 4. Univariate, Bivariate & Multivariate Analysis

### Univariate Analysis

Analysis involving **one variable**.

Example:

* Total Sales
* Total Orders
* Average Profit

A KPI/card can be used to display a single important measure.

### Bivariate Analysis

Analysis involving **two variables**.

Example:

**Sales by Country**

* Country
* Sales

### Multivariate Analysis

Analysis involving **more than two variables**.

Example:

**Sales + Profit + Orders + Customer Segment**

Scatter charts can be useful for multivariate analysis by using fields such as X-axis, Y-axis, Size and Legend.

---

## 🎨 5. Legends

A legend helps divide data into different categories and makes different groups easier to identify.

### Example

A sales chart can use Customer Segment as a legend:

* Consumer
* Corporate
* Home Office

Each category can then be visually distinguished.

---

## 📉 6. Line Chart

Line charts are mainly used to identify **trends and changes over time**.

### Examples

* Sales by month
* Revenue by year
* Profit by quarter
* Orders by date

They help identify whether a value is:

* Increasing
* Decreasing
* Stable

---

## 🔲 7. Small Multiples

Small multiples divide a visual into multiple smaller charts based on a category.

### Example

Sales by month can be divided into separate charts for:

* Consumer
* Corporate
* Home Office

This makes it easier to compare trends across different groups.

---

## 💬 8. Tooltip

A tooltip provides **additional information when hovering over a data point**.

It does not change the main visual.

### Example

A Sales chart may display:

* Sales
* Profit
* Orders
* Profit Margin

when the user hovers over a particular data point.

---

## 🔻 9. Funnel Chart

Funnel charts are useful for showing **stages in a process**.

### Example

Sales Funnel:

**Leads → Qualified Leads → Proposal → Negotiation → Won**

It helps identify where the largest drop-off occurs in a process.

---

## 🌊 10. Waterfall Chart

A waterfall chart shows how a starting value changes through a series of positive and negative changes until reaching a final value.

### Example

**Revenue → Discounts → Returns → Costs → Profit**

It helps understand the contribution of different factors to the final value.

---

## 🔵 11. Scatter Chart

Scatter charts are mainly used to analyze the relationship between numerical variables.

### Example

* X-axis → Sales
* Y-axis → Profit
* Size → Orders
* Legend → Customer Segment

### Size

The size of each data point represents the impact or magnitude of another numerical measure.

### Play Axis

The Play Axis can be used to show how data points change over another dimension, such as time.

---

## 🥧 12. Pie & Donut Charts

Pie and donut charts are useful for showing **part-to-whole relationships and percentages**.

### Example

Customer distribution by country:

* India → 40%
* USA → 30%
* UK → 20%
* Other → 10%

They are best used when there are only a limited number of categories.

---

## 🌳 13. Treemap

Treemaps are useful for showing **hierarchies and part-to-whole relationships**.

### Example

**Category → Subcategory → Product**

The size of each rectangle represents a numerical value such as Sales or Revenue.

---

## 🌍 14. Geographical Maps

Maps are used when the data contains geographical information.

### Examples

* Country
* State
* City
* Latitude
* Longitude

They can be used to analyze:

* Sales by country
* Customers by city
* Orders by region
* Revenue by state

---

## 🎯 15. KPI – Key Performance Indicator

KPI stands for **Key Performance Indicator**.

KPIs are used to monitor important business performance metrics.

### Examples

* Total Sales
* Total Profit
* Revenue
* Orders
* Profit Margin
* Sales Target Achievement

KPIs help businesses quickly understand whether they are meeting their objectives.

---

## 🎛️ 16. Slicer

A slicer is an interactive filtering visual.

Users can select values directly from the report.

### Examples

* Year
* Month
* Country
* Product
* Customer Segment

For example, selecting **2025** from a Year slicer can update multiple visuals on the report page.

---

## 🧠 Key Learning

Today's main learning was that Power BI is not only about creating charts.

The basic reporting process is:

**Clean Data → Analyze Data → Select the Right Visual → Apply Filters → Add Slicers → Format Visuals → Create Interactive Report**

Different charts should be selected according to the **business question**.
